Academics

A curriculum built for how children actually learn

Children this age learn by doing, asking and repeating — not by sitting still. Our programme is play-based and deliberately planned, so that every game in the garden and every story on the carpet is doing real academic work underneath.

Our approach

Planned play, not free-for-all

We follow the Early Years Foundation framework, adapted around the children actually in the room each year. Teachers plan in half-term blocks around a theme — Weather, Journeys, Minibeasts — and then set up the room so that children meet the learning through choice rather than instruction.

A child building a bridge in the block corner is testing balance, counting supports and negotiating with a friend. Our job is to notice that moment, name it, and stretch it with the right question.

  • Small group teaching. Focused work happens in groups of six or fewer.
  • Phonics from age four. Daily fifteen-minute sessions, synthetic phonics.
  • Maths through objects. Counters, water and cooking before worksheets.
  • Two outdoor sessions daily. The garden is a classroom, not a break.
  • Home languages welcomed. We label the room in every language our families speak.

How a topic is planned

  1. Step 1 · Observe

    Teachers note what the children are drawn to — puddles, dinosaurs, the bin lorry.

  2. Step 2 · Choose a theme

    The team picks a half-term theme that fits those interests.

  3. Step 3 · Map the learning

    Each of the six learning areas is given a clear goal within the theme.

  4. Step 4 · Set the room

    Corners, books and outdoor kit are changed to invite that learning.

  5. Step 5 · Notice & record

    Observations and photos go into each child's learning journal.

  6. Step 6 · Review with you

    We share progress and agree next steps at the termly parent meeting.

What we teach

Six learning areas

Every half-term theme touches all six. None of them is taught at a desk.

📖

Language & literacy

Talking, listening and storytelling first; then letters, sounds and early writing. Daily story time and a book to take home each Friday.

🔢

Early mathematics

Counting, sorting, pattern, shape and measure — learned through cooking, building, water play and games long before pencil and paper.

🔬

The world around us

Garden science, weather watching, growing vegetables and looking after the compost heap. Questions are more welcome than answers.

🎭

Expressive arts

Painting, clay, junk modelling, music, dance and dressing up. Process matters more than what comes home in the bag.

🤸

Physical development

Climbing, balancing and ball skills outdoors; threading, cutting and drawing indoors to build the hands that will later write.

💬

Personal & social

Sharing, turn-taking, naming feelings and solving arguments with words. The area that matters most at this age.

Year by year

What your child works towards

Goals build gently across four years. Children move at their own pace — these are signposts, not deadlines.

Caterpillars

Ages 2 – 3
  • Settle happily without a parent
  • Join in songs and rhymes
  • Speak in short sentences
  • Count and sort by colour
  • Hold a crayon and make marks
  • Play alongside other children

Chrysalis

Ages 3 – 4
  • Retell a favourite story
  • Hear the first sound in a word
  • Count reliably to ten
  • Name shapes and patterns
  • Draw with intent and explain it
  • Take turns and share with help

Explorers

Ages 4 – 5
  • Blend sounds to read simple words
  • Write their own name
  • Add and take away within ten
  • Ask questions and predict outcomes
  • Work in a small group on a project
  • Solve small disagreements with words

Butterflies

Ages 5 – 6
  • Read simple sentences aloud
  • Write a short sentence unaided
  • Number bonds and early place value
  • Measure, weigh and record findings
  • Listen and concentrate for 20 minutes
  • Manage coat, shoes and lunch alone

Assessment

No tests. Careful noticing instead.

Children here are not tested or graded. Teachers record what they observe — a photo of a first written name, a note about a child who finally asked to join a game — and build it into a learning journal that follows your child through all four years.

Twice a year we check that journal against the framework's milestones. If something is lagging, you hear it from us early and we agree a plan together. If your child needs extra support, our SENCO is involved from the first conversation, not the last.

The Butterflies year

Getting ready for big school

The final year is deliberately different. The aim is a child who walks into primary school on day one feeling capable.

✏️

Academic groundwork

Reading simple sentences, writing unaided and secure number bonds to ten — the foundations Year 1 expects.

🎒

🏫

Curriculum questions

What parents ask about academics

Is there homework?

Nothing compulsory. Butterflies take home a reading book each Friday and we suggest ten minutes of shared reading — but at this age, talking and playing together does more good than any worksheet.

My child is ahead. Will they be bored?

Teaching happens in small groups set by readiness rather than age, so a child who is ahead in phonics works with the group that suits them. Teachers stretch within an activity rather than moving children up a class.

What if my child is behind, or needs extra support?

We flag concerns early and openly. Our SENCO can put a support plan in place within the setting, and we help you access speech, language or occupational therapy referrals where they are needed.

Which curriculum framework do you follow?

The Early Years Foundation framework, covering the six learning areas above. We are inspected against it annually and our most recent report is available at the office.

Do you teach a second language?

Not formally. We sing and count in the languages our families speak at home, and label the room accordingly, but our focus at this age is depth in the language your child is learning to read in.
